Architecture explainer

Encoder–Decoder vs Decoder-only

Both are made of the same part: self-attention. The only real difference is which words each word is allowed to look at. Let's see that directly.

First, the intuition

Encoder–Decoder

Like a translator

First they read the whole English sentence — eyes darting back and forth, taking it all in. Then they write the French, one word at a time, glancing back at the original whenever they need to.

Decoder-only

Like continuing a story

There's just one line of text. You read it left to right and keep writing the next word. The “input” isn't separate — it's simply the words that came first.

The interactive below makes this concrete. Keep the analogy in mind: read-then-write with two separate parts vs. one continuous stream.

So why does it matter?

Encoder–Decoder shines when…

you transform A into B

Translation, summarization. Reading the whole input both ways first gives a complete understanding before a single output word is written. Input and output stay cleanly separate.

Decoder-only shines when…

you continue open-ended text

Chat, autocomplete, few-shot prompting. One simple stack, one rule (“predict the next word”) — and it turns out you can phrase translation and summarizing as text too, which is why most modern LLMs are this kind.

Side by side

 Encoder–DecoderDecoder-only
How input is readBoth directions, all at onceLeft to right only
Input & outputTwo separate partsOne combined stream
“Peek at input”Cross-attentionJust earlier words in the stream
Best atTranslate / summarize (A→B)Chat / continue text
ExamplesTransformer (2017), T5, BARTGPT, LLaMA, Mistral, Gemma
The big takeaway: it's the same self-attention block either way. Encoder–decoder lets the input look both ways and adds a “peek back” bridge; decoder-only keeps everything in one left-to-right line. That's the whole difference.

Lead the discussion

  • Why is it fine for the input to look both ways, but the output must only look left? (Hint: what would “looking right” mean when you're generating?)
  • A decoder-only model translates by putting the English right in the prompt. Compared to a real encoder, what might it be worse at — and why?
  • “Cross-attention” is the decoder peeking at the encoder. In a decoder-only model, what does that job instead?
